# Replays on the game server: the store, the match history and the match pages

Part of [Replays](../REPLAYS.md): the sections about what the game server keeps and what the site shows of it. Recording, the file, watching a replay and the tool are in that page.

## On the game server

The game server (`ants_server`, [`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays) has the options and the numbers) records every match that is played in one of its rooms, the games of the web pages included, and keeps the file when the match is over. Nothing is switched on by a player and nothing changes in the match: the referee's own runner is watched, as in the desktop game.

- Which matches the server keeps, how long it keeps them and which names the files hold are described in [`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays), "What is kept", "How long and how much" and "Names".
- **Who can get them.** The server's operator, with the control secret: `GET /replays` lists them, `GET /replays/<file>` gives a file and `DELETE /replays/<file>` deletes one ([`SERVER.md`](../SERVER.md#the-control-interface)). **And everybody, on the public site: the list and the files are public.** `docker-compose.stack.yml` opens the server's read-only replay door (`--replay-port`; the program's own default is off) and `docker/nginx.conf` passes `GET /replays` and `GET /replays/<file>` of the site to it: a plain GET and nothing else, with limits and a short cache for the list ([`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays)), and a plain `404` with a JSON body when the server has no such door. The list gives the newest files that this build can read, and for each the map, the players ("Green (Ann)" for a player with a name, "Green" for one without, "Red (Bot (Medium))" for a computer player), the length, whether the rules ended the match, the game version, the protocol number (`rules`) and the simulation rules number (`sim_rules`, and the list's own `sim_rules` is the serving build's). A visitor can download a file and play it with `replay_tool`, or watch it on the site (["Watching a replay"](../REPLAYS.md#watching-a-replay)).
- **Live.** The matches that run now are public too, on the same terms as the kept ones: `GET /live` lists them (every match from the moment its room starts it, the newest 50, with the same players strings as the replay list) and `GET /live/<id>` gives one as a **snapshot**, which is the match's replay file as it is at that moment. A snapshot is an incomplete file (it has no `ENDS` chunk) with one more chunk, `live`, that says how many turns the match has run ("The file"), so a page plays it with the code that plays a replay, up to the turn it has and no further; to follow the match it fetches the snapshot again and plays on. **A match with two or more people in it is shown 30 seconds late.** A watcher sees the whole map at once, so a player who watches in a second tab, or a friend who tells him, would learn what the match is doing now; the server therefore cuts the snapshot that it hands out 30 seconds behind the match (no order and no turn after that are in the bytes: a page cannot ask for more) when two or more people play it, and the page says "shown 30 seconds late". A match of computer players, or of one person with computer players, is shown as it is, about 5 seconds behind. The people are counted by the room from its seats when it starts the match, and never again. The server makes a snapshot at most once a second for a match, however many watch it (and the site's cache keeps one for a second), and when a match ends the id answers with the name of its replay file for 15 minutes, so a page that watched finds the replay. A match played with Fog of War is not shown while it runs (it would show what the fog hides). The ids hold the map and the second the match began and nothing of the room. The routes, the numbers and the limits are in [`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays).
- **A file from the server** is a file like any other of this page: `replay_tool info FILE` says what it holds, and a build plays it only when its `sim_rules` is the same as the build that recorded it (a match recorded before an update that changes how matches play out stays in the list, under "Earlier versions" on the site's page, until its recording is gone).

## The match history

A recording is kept for up to 90 days (the store gives way sooner only when it is full of people's recordings or the disk is short; the server's own matches of computer players go first: [`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays)). What the match came to is kept for good: the game server keeps one record for every match it recorded, with who won, the scores or kills, who killed and lost how many ants, how many were hatched, the bombs and the fires, and the names that the players typed. The recording can be gone and the record stays; the server never deletes a record by itself (an operator can: below). Nothing is counted while a match is played and nothing changes in a match: the numbers are the engine's own, counted when the match is over by playing the recording once more.

- **How a match is counted.** A match is played again with no screen (`replay::play()`: 0.4 - 0.5 s for a match of 10 - 12 minutes, more for a longer one) and the engine's own `MatchResult` (the one that the match's results screen is built from; `replay::Outcome::result`, with `dropped_mask` for the seats that left on the way) says what each seat came to, so the numbers are the ones a player saw at the end. This is done by the *feeder* on the server's own loop, one recording at a time, in one database transaction, in steps of about 2 ms (the file is read, its map is loaded, the turns are played a slice at a time, the record is kept; on the largest map that is played, 256 x 256, a step is as long as one turn and its state hash, 2 - 5 ms), so a match that a person plays is never slowed down by more than that, and a match with a person in it that has just ended is in the history within seconds, whatever else runs. A match of computer players alone and the older recordings (the backlog of the kept days) wait for a moment in which no room with a person in it runs or loads a match (and none is being restored after a restart); a room of computer players does not hold them: nobody waits on them. The pace follows the work: the gap after a step is counted from its end and is four times the step, so the loop is free for most of the time even while a backlog is counted. The recordings that the replay store holds when the server starts (up to 90 days of them in the stack) are counted the same way, the newest first, and so are the matches that come later: a room's own, and the files that the bot arena puts in the folder. A recording that cannot be counted is left out, once, with one line in the log (the first ten are named, the rest are counted in the summary): a map that is not on the server (or another file of the same name; a map of the players is found by its name in any letter case, as the lobby finds it), a recording that does not play out the same here. A recording of other simulation rules (`sim_rules`, ["Watching a replay"](../REPLAYS.md#watching-a-replay)) cannot be played by this build and is not counted; the summary line says how many.
- **What is kept, and where.** In the server's SQLite database ([`SERVER.md`](../SERVER.md#the-database)), three tables made by schema version 2 (and `map_plays`, the play count of each map, made by version 3 and written in the same transaction: [`SERVER.md`](../SERVER.md#the-database)): `history_matches` (one row for each match; a column and an index for everything the list filters or sorts by), `history_seats` (one row for each seat) and `history_markers` (the operator's deletes, below). The id is the recording's file name without `.antsrep`: `ants-TREASURE-20261008-143209Z`. `--no-history` keeps none and `--no-db` leaves it nothing to live in ([`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays)). A year of 40 matches a day is 15,000 rows of about 1 KiB. `history_matches.details` (nullable JSON text, 1 to 3.5 KB) holds the stats of the replay pass ("The stats of a replay pass", below); the routes give it back as `details` when it is there and a reader skips every key it does not know. Where each ant died is a field of its own in its own table, `history_deaths` (schema version 7: ["The map of deaths"](../HEATMAPS.md#the-map-of-deaths)), given back as `deaths`. If the database stops answering the history is *unavailable*, not the server: the routes answer 503, the feeder waits, one line in the log says so, the server looks again every minute and says once when it works again.
- **A match whose recording could not be made** (a file over its size limit, a turn missing) is counted all the same, from the numbers of the room's own referee: the room hands the head that it recorded and what its engine says the match came to (`replay::take_result_of`, the same reading that ends `replay::play()`) to the history, which keeps the record under an id of the form of a recording's name with a number from 9999 down (`ants-TREASURE-20261008-143209Z-9999`: no recording gets such a number, so no later file is mistaken for it), with the marker as its `details` (no pass was played) and `"recording": "removed"` (there is none). It counts as a play of its map like any other. It is the game server's own history that does this; the history service ([`HISTORY_SERVICE.md`](../HISTORY_SERVICE.md)) counts files, so such a match is in its database only through the copy at its first start.
- **The mark of a heavy order rate.** A seat whose commands went over a budget has its number in `details.heavy_orders` (a list of seats, present only then; the marker has it too), from the head's field 18 of the recording that was counted.
- **The seat that sent the most into a recording that failed.** The record of a match whose recording passed its size limit (16 MiB, `replay::kMaxFileBytes`) has `details.largest_sender`, the seat (0 Green, 1 Red, 2 Blue, 3 Black) that had sent the most bytes of commands into the recording when it failed (the recorder counts them for each seat: `Recorder::largest_sender`), so that a seat that filled it is named even though the recording is gone. It is the seat with the most, not a verdict: a recording that fills up in a long match with several heavy seats names the heaviest. No other failure (a turn that never arrived) has it.
- **The record** (layout `v` 1): `id`, `ended` (seconds since 1970 UTC, the time in the recording's name), `map`, `game` (the version that recorded it), `turns`, `seconds`, `finished` (the rules ended the match; false: a player left it or the room's time ran out), `quitter` (the seat whose quit ended the match, `null` when none did), `mode`, `format`, `draw`, `result`, `computers_only`, `seats` and `rows`. `mode` is the room's game mode, `"highest-score"` (the original's game) or `"187"` (network protocol 17, [`GAMEPLAY.md`](../GAMEPLAY.md#187-a-game-type-of-the-remake)). `format` is how the match began: `1v1` (two seats), `2v1` and `2v2` (a pair as a team against a seat, or against another pair), `ffa3` and `ffa4` (free for all; a team that the roster could not make is free for all too); a match of one seat is `solo` and has no winner. `result` is `draw`, `cut` (a player left, the time ran out, or the recording stops before the rules ended the match) or `decided`. A seat is `{"seat" 0 - 3, "color" ("Green", "Red", "Blue", "Black"), "colour" (the same value: the older spelling of the key, see the note below), "name" (what the player typed, `""` when nothing was typed: the readers show the color), "bot" (a computer player's level, `"Easy"`, `"Medium"`, `"Hard"`, or `"Bot"` when its name says none, `""` for a person), "score", "killed", "lost", "hatched", "bombs_planted", "bombs_defused", "fires_lit", "left", "winner"}`. A row is one line of the results screen, `{"seats" [one or two], "score", "killed", "lost", "hatched", "winner"}`, an alliance's numbers added up; the rows are ranked, and the first is the winner unless the match is a draw. The food and bridge counters of the engine are never fed (they are in the state hash) and are not kept. **Mode 187 has no food and no score:** the engine's score there is the kills, so a record keeps the kills as `score` (the ranking value, always equal to `killed`) and the pages must not show it as a score.
- **Who won.** The rule depends on the game mode, and every record says which one it was played by. **Highest score** (the original's game): the winner is decided by the **score**; a tie in score goes to the **kills** (enemy ants killed); equal kills too is a **draw**. Losses and hatched ants never break a tie. Rules number 8: a match that ended because one side was left and no food could score (`MatchResult::tie_standing`, [`GAMEPLAY.md`](../GAMEPLAY.md#the-last-side-standing)) is ranked as above (the highest score wins, even with no ant left), except that the side that is left wins a tie for the top score before the kills are looked at, and that tie is no draw. **187:** the last side with ants or eggs wins outright, whatever the kills (the engine's own end rule, `MatchResult::standing`); when nobody is left alone (the clock ran out, or every side ran out at once) the **most kills** wins, and equal kills is a **draw**. An alliance is one side whose numbers are added up, and both its seats are marked `winner`. A side whose **quit ended the match** is last whatever it scored (as on the screen) and the other side wins, never a draw; a seat that dropped out of the match before the end (60 seconds without a sign of life, or a quit that did not end the match) has no row and is marked `left`. Nobody is `winner` in a draw. **How this maps onto the engine's ranking:** `MatchResult::rows()` supplies which seats make a row (an alliance both confirm is one row) and puts the quitter last and, in 187, the side left first, but it ranks by score only and breaks a tie by the seat of the screen (the original gives the tie to the local team; a record has no local team, so it would be the lower seat) and knows no kills. The rule above is therefore written out in `rank_history_rows()` (`history_record.cpp`), which re-ranks the engine's rows (a stable sort, so equal rows keep the engine's order) and decides the draw; the tests make up the engine's result for every tie, in both modes (`test_history_record`). A match with no person in it (`computers_only`) is the server's own bot match or an arena file; "Bot (Hard)" in a recording's name for a seat is a computer, and a person who types exactly that is counted as one too.
- **The recording's state.** Every answer says whether the recording can still be watched: `"recording": "watch"` (the file is kept and this build plays it, and `"file"` names it), `"old"` (kept, but recorded by other simulation rules: it is listed under "Earlier versions" of the watch page and cannot be played by this build; `"file"` too) or `"removed"` (the kept days are over, or an operator deleted the file; no `"file"`; a file that is kept but damaged is reported this way too, because the public door cannot give it either). The state is not stored: it is whether the file is in the replay store's list. The answers also say `keep_days` (30), the days a recording is kept.
- **Asking for it.** On the public door, and on the control interface behind the secret: `GET /history` lists, `GET /history/<id>` gives one record. The list is `{"history": [...], "count" (the records that fit the filters), "total" (all of them), "offset", "limit", "keep_days", "sim_rules"}`, newest first, each entry a record without the bombs and fires of its seats, with `recording` and `file`. The query holds the filters of a page, each key at most once: `limit` (1 - 100, default 50), `offset`, `sort` (`new`, `old`, `score`, `kills`, `hatched`, `long`, `short`; a match's score, kills or hatched is the best of its seats', a tie keeps the newer match first), `format` (`all`, `1v1`, `team`, `ffa`), `result` (`all`, `decided`, `draw`, `cut`), `who` (`all`, `people`, `computers`), `rec` (`all`, `watch`, `old`, `removed`), `color` (`all` or a seat that took part: `green`, `red`, `blue`, `black`), `mode` (`all`, `highest-score`, `187`) and `q` (part of a map's name or of a name, at most 40 characters, any case; a seat with no typed name is searched by its color and a computer by `Bot (Level)`; `%`, `_` and `\` in the text are themselves, not wildcards). The words are lower case and exactly as written here (`color=black`, not `Black`). **The older spelling.** The filter was `colour=` and each seat of a record had a `"colour"` key before the project wrote `color` everywhere; so that bookmarks, scripts and pages in a browser's cache go on working, the server still accepts `colour=` (the same filter, the same words; `color=` and `colour=` together, or either one twice, is a 400 that names `color`) and still writes `"colour"` next to `"color"` in every seat, with the same value. The pages read `color` and fall back to `colour` only when a server of an earlier release sends no `color`. The site and the game server are one stack that is rolled out together (`docker-compose.stack.yml`), so the server rolls out with (or before) the pages; a page that asks `color=` of a server of the earlier release would get a 400. A later release removes the older spelling; use `color`. A word that is not one, a key twice or an unknown key is a 400 that names the key; nothing is guessed, and nothing a request says is ever part of the SQL. **A list cannot hold up the server:** the database runs on the server's one main thread, so a visitor's list is *time boxed* (15 ms; the operator's 100 ms) and the public door has a *budget* of query time (about 50 ms for each second, 100 ms in a burst); a list that runs into its box is a 503 `that search is too heavy for the server: narrow it`, one with a query that is asked when the budget is used up a 503 `the match history is busy: try again in a moment`, answered without asking the database (the list with no query at all is not held back by the budget, only by its box). The site's nginx adds a low allowance and a short cache for lists with a query ([`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays)). A name is printable ASCII (the protocol's rule: the game makes a `?` of any other character), so a search needs no more than that; a byte above 0x7E in a query is a 400. The item is the whole record with `recording`, `file` and `keep_days` added; an id that cannot be a match, or one that is not in the history, is 404. The routes and limits are in [`SERVER_REPLAYS.md`](../SERVER_REPLAYS.md#replays).
- **Taking one out.** Only an operator: `DELETE /history/<id>` on the control interface. If the recording is still there a marker row stays (in `history_markers`) so that the match is not counted again from it; the marker is deleted by the hourly look when the recording is gone too. Without a recording nothing stays. The public door cannot delete.
- **The stats of a replay pass** (`details`). The play that counts a match (`replay::play()`) also counts what the engine does not total: `ants_replay/match_stats` is a collector that `play()` is given as its hooks (`Hooks::before_command` for the orders, `Hooks::after_tick`, called after every tick before the engine's queues are cleared, for the rest). It only watches, through the engine's public readers (the counters, the world after every tick, the record that the engine keeps of an ant that is gone, with the team of its last damage, the bombs and fire walls on the map), so a match plays out in the same hashes with or without it (`test_history_details`) and the totals never depend on it; no engine code is touched. It counts the score and the kills of every seat every 5 seconds; who killed whom and when; the hit points each seat took and dealt; the raids of Thieves on hills; the power-ups taken; the food carried home and the points banked; the bombs that went off; the bridges built and dug away, the fires put out and the raids that got home with their loot (key `special`, counted by watching the world: "Special ants" below); when a seat had no ant (an egg hatches one for free) or was out; and the orders of every seat by minute and by kind (move, attack, bomb, fire, bridge, raid, other special order, stop, hatch, team) and by the type of ant they name. `ants_server/history_details` makes the JSON of it (layout `v` 1: its header comment lists every key), at most `kDetailsMaxBytes` (5,000, whatever the match: the series have at most 48 points, the lists of kills and raids at most 30 events, and a text that is still too long loses the lists, then points, then combat and economy, and one that is still too long is the marker below) and `GET /history/<id>` gives it under `details` (the list leaves it out). The feeder writes it in the same transaction as the record; a record that was counted before the pass existed gets it when its recording is still kept and plays here (the backfill: after the new files, newest first, at the same pace); a recording that is read and cannot be played (it does not play out here, or the pass cannot count it) leaves the marker `{"v":1,"n":1}` so that it is not tried again, and the page says only the totals were kept; one that could not be tried at all (its map is not in the maps folder or the community folder, or the replay store would not give it) leaves nothing and is tried again when the server starts again. The totals of a match are written whatever the pass does. What the pass cannot tell (it needs an event sink in the engine, outside the state hash, which is a later change): what caused each hit (a blow, a bomb, a fire wall) and which ant struck. An older recording (other simulation rules), a removed one and a live match have no stats.
- **The names stay.** The names that players typed are public wherever the public door is on, as in the recordings ("On the game server"), and a record keeps them for good, not for 90 days. The pages that tell players what is kept must say so before they show the history (the front page footer and the two name cards say it: "Recordings are kept for 90 days. Results and names are kept for good and are public.").

## The match pages

The history above is what two pages of the site show (`web/matches.html`, `web/match.html`; their look is `web/matches.css`, the code that they share `web/history_page.js`). They read the server's public door and the counts door of "Plays and likes" below, and nothing else (`GET /history`, `GET /history/<id>`, `GET /maps`, `GET /live`, and a recording only when it is asked for), they need no account to look, and every name that a record or the door carries is put in as text, never as markup. The addresses: `/matches` (the old `/matches.html` still answers, and names `/matches` as canonical; the old `/watch.html` is a 301 to it), `/match?id=<id>` (the id is the recording's file name without `.antsrep`); `/live` and `/history` are the game server's own JSON routes, so the pages cannot have those names. The matches that run now are listed on the front page (below the open rooms, each with a red **Live** mark and Watch); the old `/live-now` and `/live.html` are a 301 to `/`. A "Matches" link is in the footer of the front page and the game page and in the bar at the top of the two pages, and a "Matches" button in the header of the front page and of the game page.

- **Every match** (`/matches`) is the list of `GET /history`, newest first, 25 to a page, as lean cards: the map's picture (the six originals' own; a map of the players has the picture of the map list, `GET /maps`, or else the address that its name makes, `AntsMapPicker.thumbFor`, which the list corrects when it comes, see [PLAY_MATCH_SETUP.md](../PLAY_MATCH_SETUP.md#choosing-a-map) "Pictures"; the dark square when there is none) and name, the format (1 v 1, 2 v 1, 2 v 2, Free for all; a pill for mode 187 only, the original's game being the default), "Cut short" or "Draw", the day and time in the visitor's own time zone, the length, and one box for each **side** (the record's rows: an alliance that both seats confirm is one side, whatever the format said at the start) with the names as stored ("Pip (bot, Hard)", "Bot (Hard)" for a computer player that an earlier version stored with no name, the color word where nothing was typed), its score ("12 kills" in mode 187: its score is the kills) and **Won** on the winner. The state of the recording is on the card: **Replay**, **Older version** (kept, recorded by other rules, cannot be played by this build) or **Replay removed**. The whole card is the link (**Stats & replay**, or **Stats** when there is no replay to show). Over the cards: a search (a map or a name; the server's own `q`, at most 40 printable ASCII characters), **All matches / With people / Computers only**, and Format, Result, Replay, Color, Mode and Sort by (the server's words, `docs/REPLAYS.md` "The match history"; a phone keeps the search, the three buttons and the sort in view and the rest behind **Filters**), with a chip for every filter that is not the default and **Clear all**. A sort by a count (score, kills, hatched) shows that count on each card (in mode 187, whose score is the kills, a sort by score shows the kills). The address is the state (`?who=people&format=ffa&sort=kills&q=pip&page=3`: only what is not the default, in the server's words), so a link can be sent and Back works; the page is not loaded again for a filter. **What it asks the server:** the plain `GET /history` (the newest 50, cached by the site, not held to the server's budget) for the first two pages of the default view, and one query for every filter, search, sort and later page (`limit=25&offset=...`: the site allows two of them a second for each address, so a search waits 450 ms after the last key, a choice of a select, a button or a page waits 350 ms and the choices that come within that time are one request and one step of the history, and a look that is late for a newer one is dropped). A busy server (503) or one with no history (404) is said in the server's own words with a **Try again**, and a page past the last one (an old link) says so with **Back to the first page**. A button **N live now** is in the header while a match is being played (one look when the page opens).
- **One match** (`/match?id=<id>`) shows the whole record. **The result:** the map, the format, the day and the length, who won and the score of every side, and **Show replay**, **Download file** and **Copy link**. **The scoreboard:** a score-share bar (a segment for each side), and for every seat the Score, Killed, Lost and Hatched with a bar for the size of each against the best seat, "Won" and "Left early" marks, and the total of a side of two (a phone has a card for each seat and each side of two instead of the table). Mode 187 has no Score column. **About this match:** the map and its size, the mode, who played (people and computer players), the format, the length and the turns, when it ended, how it ended (the rules, cut short, a quit), the version that recorded it, and the id. A recording of an older version has no button, only the words (who made it and that this version cannot play it) and **Download file**; one that is gone says "Replay removed: recordings are kept for 90 days. The stats below stay." (a match that is not in the history, for instance one that ended a moment ago and is not counted yet, says so and offers its replay and the list.) The page is a list of **sections**, each a panel that is built from the record or is nothing (`SECTIONS` in `match.html`). The stats of the replay pass are four of them (`web/match_stats.js` and `match_stats.css`, which read the record's `details` through `AntsHistory.detailsOf`: a plain object or nothing, every number read as a number, every seat as 0 to 3, every name as text): a **band of highlights** under the result (up to four chips that link to their section: first blood, the lead, the comeback, the busiest seat, or "No ant was lost", said only when the scoreboard also has no ant lost, since an ant that died with no team to blame is in no kill; no chip links to a section that is not on the page, so the raids chip of the picture comes with the Combat section), **Special ants** (right under the scoreboard, below), **Over time** (the score of every seat and the kills so far as charts of inline SVG with no library, a shape for each seat besides its color, a strip of who had the most on the score box with a side of two named and two-toned, first blood marked, and the facts under them: last kill, changes of the lead, the comeback, the best run of kills, a seat that had no ant left) and **Activity** (a strip of squares of the orders of every seat in every minute, the busiest minute of each ringed, a table of the orders by kind, the busiest seat and minute). A match with no `details` (an older version, a recording that was removed before it was counted, a match that has just ended, a pass that could not count it) has none of the other three and one line under the scoreboard that says why ("More stats"), and its Special ants show the numbers that the seats' rows hold; **Where ants died** ("The map of deaths", below) is the card between "Over time" and "Activity"; the older-version line under the result says that the totals are complete. The rest of the numbers for combat (who killed whom, the hit points of each team on each seat, the list of kills and of raids) and for the economy (the points banked, the cost of hatching, the types of ant used in orders) are kept in `details` and have no section yet; every ant that hatches is a Worker and the game has no bomb throw, so there is no "types hatched" and no "thrown".
- **Special ants** (`web/match_stats.js`, under the scoreboard). A card for each type of ant that somebody had (Swimmer, Fire Ant, Bomber, Thief), a row for each seat in the scoreboard's order (dim when the seat had none of the type) and a bar for each number against the best seat; a type that nobody used is left out with one line ("No Swimmer or Thief took a power-up or did anything in this match."; a level may start its ants as a type, so the page does not say that nobody had one), and a card **Food and fighting** (food trips, Combat Ants, damage dealt and taken) closes it. Each card has *Ants* (how many of the seat's ants took the power-up and became the type) and: Swimmer, **built** and **taken down**; Fire Ant, **lit** and **put out**; Bomber, **set**, **defused** and **went off**; Thief, **raids** ("5 of 8": home of tried) and **loot**. *Built* is water squares bridged through the fourth stage (a half built bridge is none, nor is one that came back after a dig that was cut short); *taken down* is finished bridges dug away to the end (a bridge that falls by itself after 3 minutes is not counted); *put out* is fire walls put out by the seat's Fire Ants, whoever lit them (a fire that burns out is not counted); a raid is *tried* when a Thief goes into an enemy hill (the raid clip starts) and *home* when its loot reached the Thief's own hill (a hill with no points, and a Thief killed on the way, are tried and never home; a hill below zero gives a debt, which comes home too); *went off* is its bombs that any ant stepped on. Fires lit and bombs set and defused are the seat's row; the ants of each type, the bombs that went off, food and damage are `details.economy` and `details.combat`; bridges, fires put out and raids are the key `special` (`{"built", "undug", "out": [x4], "raids": [[tried, home, loot] x4]}`), which the pass counts by watching the world (`ants_replay/match_stats`: the engine's own `bridges_built` is never raised, and nothing in the simulation changes); it is about 150 bytes whatever the match, so it stays when a long match loses its combat and economy and goes only when nothing else would fit. A match counted before these counts has no `special`: the page shows what the record has and hides what it lacks (an older match shows the raids as "8 tried", counted from the list of raids when that is whole), and there is no backfill. The Stats panel of the replay page keeps its small line of bombs and fires. Tests: `test_history_details` (HD1.5 and HD1.5b on a recording of four computer players on ISLANDS that has Swimmers building and digging, HD1.7, HD2.8, HD3.5), `web_match_stats_check.js`, `tests/data/special_ants_mutants.json` and the broken copies of `test_web_match_stats.py`.
- **Plays and likes** (`web/replay_likes.js`; the door, the numbers and the privacy note are in [`REPLAY_COUNTS.md`](../REPLAY_COUNTS.md)). A card and the result of a match page show how often the replay was played ("▶ 12", "12 plays", "1 play") and how many people like it (a heart with its count; a zero is dim). A logged-in person ([`HUB.md`](../HUB.md)) taps the heart to like and again to take it back: it changes at once, the count that the door answers is the one shown, and a call that fails puts it back with one plain line ("Slow down a little.", "Liking isn't available right now.", "Couldn't reach the server."). Nobody sees who liked; the page asks only which of the shown matches *you* like (one look for the cards of a page). Not logged in, a tap shows "Log in to like a replay." with **Log in with Discord** and asks for nothing. A recording that is gone or older keeps its numbers and its heart is a plain count. **Most watched** and **Most liked** are the server's `sort=plays` and `sort=likes`; the length stays the big number and the count of the sort is the bright one.
- **The replay in the page.** **Show replay** opens the game page itself in a frame (`/play?replay=<file>&embed=1`, the page's mode with the picture and the bar only: no header, no footer, no stats, no way out of the page) and **only then** is anything of the game fetched (the wasm and its data, about 9 MB the first time, which the browser keeps; and the recording): the page makes the frame in one place, `setReplay`, when it opens, and **Hide replay** (or the game's own Quit inside the frame, which tells the page; the frame's address opened on its own has no page to tell, and its Quit goes to the list) takes it away and frees the game; there is one frame at most. **Fullscreen** (where the browser has it for an element) and **Own page** (a new tab) are in the replay's header. The page and the frame are one origin and carry the two cross-origin isolation headers that every page of the site carries (`Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin`, `Cross-Origin-Embedder-Policy: require-corp`, [`docker/nginx.conf`](../../docker/nginx.conf): a new `location` with an `add_header` of its own drops the ones of the server, so each repeats them), which is what lets the frame run under the page; a phone's frame is small (turn the phone, or use Fullscreen).
- **The matches that run now** are on the front page ([`HOME_SCREEN.md`](../HOME_SCREEN.md)), under the open rooms: the server's `GET /live` every 5 seconds while the list shows (and at once when a room has gone from the list of games: it may have started its match), each match from its start, with a red **Live** mark, the map, who plays, the time it started, how long it has run (**Starting…** until its first turn) and **Watch** (`/play?live=<id>`, "Watching a replay" below). A running match has no stats (the door gives only the recording so far, which the game page plays; its scores are in the picture); when it ends it is counted into the history by the feeder and its card appears in the list (and a match page that is asked for before that says so). The bar at the top of the other pages shows a small red "N live" mark, only while at least one match runs, that links to the front page ([`SITE_NAV.md`](../SITE_NAV.md)).
- **The stats on the replay page.** The game page in replay mode (not a match that is being played, not the frame of a match page, and only a recording of the server's own store) asks `GET /history/<id>` once and, when the server has the record, gets a **Stats** button in the header (wide screens; a phone has a row under the controls instead) that opens a panel with the winner, the numbers of every seat, the total of a side and a link to the match page. It is closed at first, and its closed row (a phone's too) says nothing of the result, so it does not tell who won before the viewer asks; the numbers are the final ones: they do not follow the picture. The panel is `AntsHistory.replayStats` and `web/replay_stats.css` (the file also holds the frame mode's rules); under its table it holds the small card **Where ants died** when the match has one ("The map of deaths", below).
- **The numbers of the results screen, counted live.** The same pass runs while a match is played, on the confirmed engine (`ants_app/live_match_stats`: a game on one machine after each of its ticks, a match of the network from the lock-step runner's tap for every turn it executes, live, in a catch-up and in a rejoin from its first turn), and `make_match_report` (`ants_app/match_report`) makes of it the screen's numbers when the match is over: damage done and taken, food trips and points, bombs set, fires lit, raids and loot, the honors and the Play of the game. It counts no orders and takes nothing from the engine's queues. A tick or turn it did not see, or a match that was not played from its first turn makes `ok` false, and the screen shows the original's numbers only (`test_match_report`: live equals the recording's pass, field by field).
- **The honors and the play** (`make_match_report`). **Winning side**: the seats of the first row of the results. **MVP**: of that side the seat with the highest average of its shares of the roster's damage done, kills and points banked (what the hill gained, a thief's loot included, so loot is not counted twice; thousandths, each rounded down, a thing nobody did left out; ties to the lower seat; the card says "62% of the play"). **First blood**: the first kill by a team that is not the victim's own (a drowning is nobody's). **Top Thief**: the seat with the most raids whose loot got home (`raids_home` of the stats pass: a raid on a hill with nothing, and a Thief killed on the way, are begun and never home; more than none; the lower seat on a tie; the card says the raids and their points, `loot_home`); in 187 **Last stand** instead: the last seat off the winning side that ran out of ants for good. **Play of the game**: the best moment of one seat (`ants_app/match_report.cpp` has the same definitions in its header comment). A moment is a seat's own 10 seconds: its kills from one kill to the last that is less than 200 turns after it, so every kill begins one; a kill is one of another seat's ants that the seat killed (First blood's kills: not a drowned ant, not one that no team did). Points: **10** a kill, and for the kills that mattered **+20** the kill that knocked a seat out (the last ant that the seat lost before it was out of the match, `out_turn`: no ant, no egg, no hatch; a seat that came back from an egg was not out then; a drowned last ant is nobody's), **+15** a Thief that was raiding the killer's own hill killed within 4 tiles of its mound (the 4 by 4 tiles of the hill: the hill is saved; the distance is the larger of the two tile differences, 0 on the mound; the Thief's target team is the engine's, from the order to raid to the end of the raid clip that takes the loot), **+10** a Thief that carried the loot of a raid when it was killed, wherever, and **+15** a group wiped out (a group is the ants of one seat within 4 tiles of each other; a kill wipes one out when no other ant of its victim's seat is left within 4 tiles of the victim after the tick and, with that kill, at least 3 ants of that seat that the same seat killed in the moment lay within 4 tiles of it; once for a place in a moment, and the ants must fall inside it). The moment with the most points is the Play; ties go to the earlier moment (its first kill), then the lower seat. There is no minimum and no bonus for speed: one kill is a play when it is the only one; none when no seat killed anything. The play says the seat, its kills, the seconds from the first kill to the last (rounded up, at least 1 for two kills or more, 0 for one), the bonuses that it has and the first seat that it knocked out; its time and place (Watch opens the replay five seconds before) are the first kill's; what the card says of it is in [`VIEW_AND_HUD.md`](../VIEW_AND_HUD.md#the-new-look-of-the-results-screen). For the Thief, the loot and the ants near a kill the pass keeps three facts on each `Kill` (`raid_on`, `carried_loot`, `mates_near`) and the tile of each hill (`hill_x`, `hill_y`), read from the world beside everything else it counts; the kill's cause (a blow, a bomb, a fire) is not told: the pass cannot tell it.
